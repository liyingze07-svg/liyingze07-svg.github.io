Yingze Li 李映泽

Building systems that make
AI research and inference more efficient.

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at Harbin Institute of Technology, advised by Prof. Hongzhi Wang. I am currently a research intern at the Shanghai Academy of AI for Science (SAIS), working with Dr. Taoli Cheng on recursive self-improvement (RSI) for scientific reasoning models.

From September 2025 to September 2026, I was a visiting researcher at the National University of Singapore, working with Prof. Bingsheng He (IEEE Fellow) and directly mentored by Prof. Yao Chen (now at HUST).

My research connects self-improving research agents, language-model efficiency, and learning-based data systems. At SAIS, I am exploring how agent-driven generation, verification, and feedback-guided post-training can improve scientific reasoning models.

Research

01

Self-improving research agents

AutoResearch / RSI · Since Feb 2026

I build research-agent infrastructure for idea exploration and iterative analysis, with human verification of key claims. My current research at SAIS extends this direction toward RSI for scientific reasoning models.

  • NeurIPS 2026 (CCF A / CORE A*, first author): the workflow supported problem exploration and theorem design and analysis; I handled human verification and writing.
  • Findings of EMNLP 2026 (second author): I contributed research-agent tools, collaborative idea exploration, data analysis, and writing discussions.
02

Efficient LLM analytics

SOLO · ICML 2026 · CCF A / CORE A*

Cache-aware row and column reordering for LLM-augmented table analytics. Aligning data layout with prefix reuse reduces repeated prefill computation.

Up to 90.3% higher prefill throughput under the evaluated fixed cache budget.

Read the paper
03

Learning-based queries

SMILE · SIGMOD 2026 · CCF A / CORE A*

Small, column-specific language models generate candidates for approximate LIKE queries. Data-side verification and fallback execution balance efficiency and recall.

1.18–9.51× query speedup over the next-best method in the reported benchmarks.
